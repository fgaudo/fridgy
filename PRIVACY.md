# Fridgy privacy policy

Last updated: 28 October 2026.

This policy applies to Fridgy, the Android app with the package name `net.gaudo.fridgy`, published by Francesco
Gaudenzi (fgaudo@gmail.com). It says what the app does with your data. Fridgy is free software: its source code is
at <https://github.com/fgaudo/fridgy>, and anyone can check that it does what this page says.

## In short

Fridgy has no account, no analytics, no advertising and no crash reporting. The developer receives nothing from the
app. The only data that leaves your phone is a barcode you scan, sent to Open Food Facts to find the product's
name.

## What the app stores on your phone

The products you enter (a name and an expiration date), and the product names that Open Food Facts answered for
the barcodes you scanned, which the app keeps so as not to ask again. They are stored in the app's private storage
on the phone, which no other app can read. Deleting a product removes it; uninstalling the app removes everything.

If your phone backs up its apps to your Google account (Settings › System › Backup), Android may include Fridgy's
data in that backup, as it does for every app. That backup is Google's service, under
[Google's privacy policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy), and you turn it on or off in the phone's settings.

## The barcode scanner

The scanner uses the camera to read a barcode from the package. The reading happens on the phone: no picture is
stored or sent anywhere. The camera is optional: without it, or when you deny the permission, you type the name.

## The lookup on Open Food Facts

When you scan a barcode, the app asks the Open Food Facts database (<https://openfoodfacts.org>) for the product
with that barcode, to fill in its name. That request carries the barcode, the app's name and version with the
developer's contact email, as Open Food Facts asks every app to identify itself, and, like any request on the
internet, your phone's IP address. Open Food Facts is run by a French non-profit; what it does with the requests it
receives is in [its privacy policy](https://world.openfoodfacts.org/privacy). Typing a name sends nothing.

## Permissions

- **Camera**: only for the scanner, only while its dialog is open.
- **Internet**: only for the lookup on Open Food Facts.

## Your data

The developer holds no data about you, so there is nothing to ask for, correct or delete on the developer's side.
Your products are yours, on your phone: delete them in the app, or uninstall it.

## Changes

Changes to this policy are made in the app's repository, where its history can be read; the date at the top is
the one of the last change.
